What you are looking at

  • Orange means real money, and nothing else does. Paper trading is amber, the interactive accent is blue, and red is reserved for loss and danger. An accent in the red family would make "this control is available" and "you are losing money" the same colour, so the palette rules that out at the token level.
  • Arming takes two actions, not one. Lift the cover, then throw the switch. The covers are not theatre: they are there because the failure mode for an autonomous trading system is a single careless tap, and a control that can be armed by accident will eventually be armed by accident.
  • Every number carries the sentence that explains it. The prose inside each tile is not a tooltip — it is the reason the figure can be checked rather than trusted. Open one and it tells you how it was derived and what would make it wrong.
  • It reports what it cannot do. The options lane is armed and still cannot fire, because spendable cash is below its floor. Stated plainly, because a silent no-op is indistinguishable from a broken component.
  • The refusals are the product. Declines is not an error log. It is the record of every trade the system chose not to make, with its reason and its grade — including the ones where declining turned out to be wrong.
  • Read-only, over a private network. The real cockpit reaches its operator on their own tailnet behind a PIN. It is not on the public internet, and this page is a rebuild rather than a window into it.

Where the real one lives

The cockpit runs on the owner's own machine, reachable only over the owner's private network. There is no hosted version, no shared instance, and no account of yours for us to hold.
