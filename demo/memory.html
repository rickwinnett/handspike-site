Demo · the memory system

The saddlebags. Every install earns its own.

Sovereign keeps a memory: a file of scars, written on your machine, from your own trades. Each entry records what went wrong and the rule it produced — and the harness consults those rules before the mind reasons. Nothing about it is hosted, synced, or shared.

The software you install is identical to everyone else's. What it becomes is not. Every venue quirk it hits, every accounting flattery it catches, every silent failure it survives gets written down — and stays with your copy.

It works. Filter by kind, then open an entry to see the scar and the rule it produced. These five are real: earned by the founder's own installations running founder capital, and either already published in the field report or harmless to share.

Leveraged tickers were mechanically dead below the venue's $2,000 equity floor — silently. The strategy looked flat for two weeks; the venue was refusing every order the whole time. Nothing errored, nothing alarmed, and the flat line was indistinguishable from a strategy that had simply stopped finding trades.

The rule it produced The harness checks venue eligibility gates before the mind reasons about a ticker, and a refusal is recorded with its reason rather than swallowed.

A sibling strategy reported +84.8% on mid-price paper fills. Re-run with real quotes and real commissions: −2.3%. The entire "edge" was the spread it never paid and the fees it never counted.

The rule it produced Paper books fill at the real ask, sell at the real bid, and pay the published fee. A book that flatters itself is worse than no book, because it is trusted.

The model was silent on 36% of decision cycles — quota exhaustion plus a request timeout — and nothing said so. A silent cycle writes a record with no thesis, which on readback is indistinguishable from a deliberate decision to wait. The system was reading its own outages as patience.

The rule it produced Every model call is attributed and logged, and the silence rate is computed from the ledger and shown back to the mind. A silent mind is an alarm, not a quiet day.

The venue's keyless API reports volume and open interest as null for every market, and liquidity as zero — including on markets that had traded thirty seconds earlier. A liquidity filter built on those fields silently rejected the entire exchange and returned zero candidates, which looks exactly like "nothing worth trading today".

The rule it produced Activity is measured from the public trade tape instead of the quote fields, and an empty scan reports its funnel — how many candidates died at each stage — so a zero-result run is diagnosable rather than a shrug.

The venue reports spot USD and futures buying power through different endpoints, and they are the same dollars seen twice — futures draws on the spot balance with no sweep between them. Adding the two reported $319.43 against about $186 of real money. A balance that double-counts is not a display bug; it is a sizing input.

The rule it produced Each venue has exactly one authoritative balance field per question, named in the code with the measurement that established it. Balances are never summed across endpoints.

Entries are plain files on the operator's own disk. There is no memory service, no account, and nothing to sync — which is also why nobody can hand you someone else's.

Why memory, not updates

Improvement doesn't arrive from us. It accrues on your machine.

The usual way software gets better is the vendor pushing updates. For a trading harness that would mean us pushing strategy — every customer's behavior converging on one vendor's opinion, and every customer trusting that opinion with their money. We decline both halves of that bargain.

Sovereign is built to get better the other way. When something goes wrong — a venue rule nobody documented, a data feed lying politely, a book flattering itself — the lesson becomes a memory entry, and the entry becomes a check the harness runs before the model spends a single token of reasoning. We ship the rails and the memory machinery. The seasoning comes from your own data, on your own server, and it never leaves.

What an entry buys you

  • A rule, not a story — each scar ends in a check the harness enforces deterministically, outside the model's control. The memory is executable caution.
  • Loaded before reasoning — eligibility gates, data-truth checks, and accounting rules run first, so the mind never wastes judgment on a question the scars already answered.
  • Yours alone — entries are written from your fills, your venue, your outages. They describe your operation, which is why no vendor could have shipped them.
  • Designed to compound — the longer an install runs, the more of its failure surface is fenced. Not a promise of profit; a shrinking inventory of ways to be surprised.

The part no one can copy

A pirated Sovereign is a newborn. Yours is a veteran.

Clone the code and you get the rails — and empty saddlebags. Every entry above was paid for in real time and real tuition on one operator's machine, and that is where it lives. A copy of Sovereign starts from zero scars; a year-old install carries a year of them. The product we sell is the harness. The product you end up owning is the veteran.
